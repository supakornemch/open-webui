# Data Strata
## A Design Philosophy for Infrastructure Visualization

**Space is the primary information system.** Every cloud resource, every network hop, every data flow exists not as a node in a diagram but as a geological layer — strata compressed by time and traffic. The composition reveals depth through vertical separation: the user floats above, the proxy mediates at the center, the intelligence burns below. Horizontals are for time (request → response), verticals are for abstraction (surface → depth). White space is not empty — it is the medium through which services breathe. Every pixel of silence between components has been measured, adjusted, reconsidered.

**Color as material, not decoration.** A limited palette drawn from infrastructure itself: the cool slate of server racks, the warm amber of network activity LEDs, the deep indigo of terminal screens at night. No more than five hues, each assigned a precise semantic role — storage, compute, network, identity, data plane. Colors do not decorate; they encode. The palette calibrated over countless iterations until the composition locks into perfect chromatic balance.

**Typography whispers architecture.** Type exists only to name what space and color have already expressed. Labels sparse, essential, positioned with surgical precision. Mono or geometric sans — the typography of engineering. A single large word anchors the composition; tiny, meticulously kerned labels float near their services. No paragraph, no legend that isn't embedded in the visual itself.

**Geometry carries meaning.** Connections are conduits, not lines. Thickness encodes bandwidth; opacity encodes latency; curvature encodes relationship nature. Service boundaries are rounded rectangles of precise corner radius — a mathematical constant applied consistently. The grid is rigid until the composition demands it break — at which point the violation is intentional, legible.

**The artifact as scientific document.** This should feel like a plate from "An Atlas of Distributed Systems" — one page in a larger taxonomy. The composition was not designed but *discovered* — the architecture organized itself into this form and the designer merely documented it with painstaking fidelity.

---

*Data Strata treats cloud architecture as a subject worthy of the same visual reverence we reserve for natural phenomena — geological cross-sections, astronomical charts, anatomical plates.*
